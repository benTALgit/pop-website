      What a captain does

      The job is the same everywhere: be the person this office hears from about child care every single week. The ask changes with where the member stands.

      With a champion

      Keep them fighting. Bring a provider story, ask for a specific action that week (a co-sponsor, a post, a word with a colleague), and thank them publicly when they do it.

      With a leader or chair

      Make the issue unavoidable. Steady, polite pressure from constituents who run child care programs, coordinated with partners who already have the relationship, never freelancing on the budget ask.

      With a likely champion

      Get the meeting, make the ask, and keep asking. A captain’s week ends with a clear answer or a date for the next touch.

      A suggested weekly rhythm

      1. Early in the week: one touch with the office’s scheduler or legislative staffer, by phone or email.
      2. Midweek: one visible moment, such as a provider visit to the district office, a call-in from three providers, a letter, or tagging the member on a post.
      3. End of week: a two-line report to the campaign (what happened, where they stand, what’s next) so this map can be updated.

      Notes on this list

      • Champions are the members listed as signed on at popchildcare.org/campaign. All of their districts are included.
      • Chairs and leadership posts are from the 2025–26 session. New chairs for 2027 are named in January.
      • Members-elect take office in January. Where a district was added from outside our notes, its card says so; confirm those members won on Nov 3 before assigning a captain.
