PAY OUR PROVIDERS
For legislative visits

New York can't build universal child care on poverty wages.

The State is expanding child care faster than ever. The providers who make it possible earn less than 96% of New York workers, and they are leaving the field. Our ask: fund the workforce and fund the families in the 2027 budget.

$1.8B
A wage scale for child care workers

Fund the CUNY-PDI / Workforce Collaborative scale (role, credentials, experience; parity with K-12) for every provider, in centers and homes, without raising what families pay or interfering with collective bargaining.

$1.2B
CCAP for every eligible family

$1.2B in new recurring funding, so no eligible family is waitlisted or turned away, and providers get fair, reliable income.

The need

$37,675median child care educator pay; less than 96% of NY jobs
$6.01median hourly take-home for NYC family child care providers
57%of NY programs short-staffed; 16,000+ kids unserved
  • Family child care capacity fell 28% from 2019 to 2024; 60% of census tracts are child care deserts.
  • In May 2026, 31 counties and NYC had stopped enrolling eligible families in CCAP for lack of funds. NYC's waitlist hit ~30,000 children.
  • The FY27 budget added $1.2B for CCAP but nothing for wages.

The principles from NYCAT

  1. A statewide wage scale and career ladder, indexed to cost of living.
  2. Pay subsidies and CCAP rates based on the true cost of care.
  3. Worker voice: sectoral bargaining, a wage board, labor peace.
  4. A phase-in backed by dedicated progressive revenue.

New York can pay for it

The Fiscal Policy Institute shows a progressive package (a small payroll tax, a corporate surcharge, and a 1% NYC tax on income over $1M) raises about $8B a year, enough for statewide universal care with providers earning $50,000 to $60,000.

Will you champion these demands? Tell leadership and the Governor you support $1.8B for the wage scale and $1.2B for CCAP in the 2027 budget, and join POP's Child Care Champions.
