PAY OUR PROVIDERS
Testify at City Council

Paid late. Paid too little. Tell the City Council.

The City Council is holding a hearing on the business of child care, and we need providers, parents and allies there, in person or on Zoom, to tell them what is really going on with late payments, unexplained payment reversals, system failures, and more.

Thu, Oct 29 1 PM In person or Zoom

250 Broadway, 8th Floor, Hearing Room 1, Manhattan. Hearing details

For child care providers every month is a crisis. Month after month, they are paid late and some are seeing their payments reversed with no explanation at all.

Already child care providers are paid only after they submit attendance for the month. Imagine an 8th grade teacher getting docked pay because students were out with the flu! Years ago, child care providers were promised that the State would begin paying in advance and finally based on enrollment, rather than attendance. None of this has happened.

Now, the city needs more child care providers to meet demand for universal child care. Will they fix these problems first? Join us to testify -- demand that they do! That's why this is part of our fight to Pay Our Providers. Pay justice is a lot of things. Provider pay is abysmally low and out of sync with what we pay other educators, which is why we're fighting for $1.8 billion for a child care wage scale and $1.2 billion for CCAP. But we can't have pay justice if people aren't even paid on time. We need wage justice, and people need to at least get paid on time.

The people in charge need to hear from folks who are outraged about this, including providers, parents and families who might not know all the technicalities but know exactly what it means when a check doesn't come. Sign up to testify, and join us.

How it works

  1. Sign up with us. Tell us how you'd like to testify, so we can get you ready and make sure POP shows up together.
  2. Register with the Council. We'll give you your answers to copy into the Council's official sign-up form. It takes about two minutes.
  3. Speak up on Oct 29. You get two minutes, in person or on Zoom. Or send written testimony, which the Council takes up to 72 hours after the hearing.

    What to say in two minutes

    • Who you are. Your name, your program or your kids' program, and your neighborhood or Council district.
    • What happened. How late, how many times, how much. Payments that were reversed. Bring your receipts.
    • What it did to you. Covering payroll or rent out of pocket, borrowing, going without your own pay, families and children who were affected.
    • What you want. Pay providers on time, every time. Pay in advance and based on enrollment, like the state committed to. And Pay Our Providers: a real wage scale for everyone who does this work.

    Everything you say or send becomes part of the public record.
