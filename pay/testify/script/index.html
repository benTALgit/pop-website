PAY OUR PROVIDERS
Testimony guide

What to say on Oct 29. Two minutes, your story.

Talking points and a fill-in-the-blank script for the City Council hearing on the business of child care. Pick the script for providers or for parents, fill in your blanks and practice it once out loud.

Thu, Oct 29 1 PM In person or Zoom
Go to the script Sign up to testify

Before you testify

You get two minutes, so pick one or two stories and tell them well. You don't need to know the policy details -- you know what it means when a payment doesn't come.

  • When: Thursday, October 29, 1 PM, City Council Subcommittee on Early Childhood Education with the Committee on Small Business, "Oversight - The Business of Child Care"
  • Where: 250 Broadway, 8th Floor, Hearing Room 1, Manhattan, or on Zoom
  • Register first: council.nyc.gov/testify. On Zoom you have to register ahead. In person you can also sign up with the Sergeant-at-Arms on the day.
  • Need interpretation? Email translationservice@council.nyc.gov by Monday, October 26.
  • Can't speak? Send written testimony up to 72 hours after the hearing.
  • Bring your receipts: payment dates, letters, screenshots, anything that shows how late, how often and how much.
  • Everything you say or send becomes part of the public record.

Talking points

Use the ones that match your story. You don't need all of them.

For many providers, every month is a crisis.

  • Month after month, providers are paid late, and some see payments reversed with no explanation at all.
  • Late payments, unexplained reversals and system failures mean providers cover payroll, rent and food out of their own pockets, borrow, or go without their own pay to keep their doors open.

We're paid only after we submit attendance.

  • Providers are paid only after they submit attendance for the month.
  • Imagine an 8th grade teacher getting docked pay because students were out with the flu. That's what attendance-based pay does to us.

The State made us a promise. None of it has happened.

  • Years ago, providers were promised the State would begin paying in advance, and finally based on enrollment, rather than attendance.
  • None of this has happened.

The City needs more of us -- fix this first.

  • The City needs more child care providers to meet demand for universal child care.
  • It won't get them if the providers we have now can't count on getting paid. Fix these problems before more providers are affected.

What we're demanding:

  • Pay providers in advance
  • Pay based on enrollment
  • Hold agencies and third-party vendors accountable -- ACS is the City agency that runs the payments and the companies it contracts to process them

This is about pay justice.

  • Our campaign to Pay Our Providers is about pay justice. Pay justice means thriving wages -- but that's not all.
  • We need the basics of on-time payment and functional payment systems too. The New York City Council must deliver on these promises.

Written testimony and tips

Written testimony can be longer than two minutes, so use the same script and add the details: dates, amounts, copies of letters. Put your name, the hearing date and "Oversight - The Business of Child Care" at the top.

Where to send it: the Council takes written testimony only through its online form, council.nyc.gov/testify. Under "How will you be testifying?" choose Submitting written testimony, pick the Oct 29 hearing, and upload your file (Word or PDF, up to 10 MB). The deadline is 72 hours after the hearing ends, so send it by Sunday, November 1 at the latest (earlier is safer, since the end time isn't fixed). Questions about the hearing go to hearings@council.nyc.gov.

  • Speak from your own life. Your story is the evidence. The numbers and dates make it hard to ignore.
  • Say it plainly. Talk like you're telling a neighbor, not reading a report.
  • End with the ask. The three demands are what we want Council members to remember.
